# H0 significance test — run `2026-08-30T02-54-42-056Z`

**Significance verdict: NULL.** No model arm is correct on more triples than
the best trivial baseline. **Separately: the gate that verdict is measured
against is unsatisfiable by construction** — see "The ceiling" below. The null
is real, and it is also uninformative about evidence utility.

This closes the one WP3/WP4 acceptance-gate item that had never been executed.
`redesign-requirements.md` §7 #5 and `work-packages.md` WP4 both require a
significance test against the best trivial baseline;
`phase-3-h0-evidence-utility/current-state.md` recorded it as never run.

Nothing was re-run against a model. This is a re-analysis of the frozen
records; the six `runs/*-2026-08-30T02-54-42-056Z.jsonl` files are unchanged,
byte for byte.

## Which test, and why

`redesign-requirements.md` §7 #4 requires "per-triple correct as a binary
vector; aggregate with a paired test against the constant baseline". §7 #5
names "Fisher exact". Those are two different tests, and the document never
reconciles them.

Both arms are evaluated on the same triples, so the observations are paired.
**McNemar's exact test is therefore binding** and Fisher's exact test is
reported as an unpaired cross-check. Discordant-pair counts here run from 1 to
14, so the paired test is the exact two-sided binomial on discordant pairs, not
a χ² approximation — the normal approximation the previous (dead) code used is
unreliable below roughly 25 discordant pairs.

- **α = 0.05**, declared in the artifact (`significance.alpha`).
- **Binding unit: the triple** (n = 30). A triple counts as correct for an arm
  when at least 2 of its 3 repeats are correct; halted and schema-invalid
  repeats count against the arm. That is the conservative direction for an arm
  claiming to beat a baseline that never halts.
- **Record unit** (n ≤ 90) is reported alongside. It ignores the clustering of
  three repeats within a triple, so it is descriptive, not binding.
- Pairs where either side is schema-invalid or ungraded are dropped from *both*
  arms, which is why record-level baseline counts differ per comparison.

## Result

Against the best trivial baseline (`baseline-compose`, 9/30 triples correct):

| unit | arm | arm correct | compose correct | delta | arm only | compose only | McNemar exact p | Fisher p |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| triple (n=30) | hunk-only | 7 | 9 | −2 | **0** | 2 | 0.5000 | 0.7710 |
| triple (n=30) | selected | 8 | 9 | −1 | **0** | 1 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| triple (n=30) | full-bundle | 4 | 9 | −5 | **0** | 5 | 0.0625 | 0.2092 |
| record (n=86) | hunk-only | 20 | 24 | −4 | **0** | 4 | 0.1250 | 0.6004 |

| record (n=89) | full-bundle | 13 | 27 | −14 | **0** | 14 | **0.0001** | 0.0189 |

Read the `arm only` column. **It is zero everywhere.** There is not one
conflict in this corpus that a model arm resolved correctly and the constant
did not. Every delta is negative. The only result that reaches significance is
full-bundle being *worse* than a constant.

The gate — "arm correct on ≥N more triples than the best trivial baseline at
exact p < 0.05" — is not met by any arm, at either unit, against any of the
three baselines.

## The ceiling: why `arm only = 0` was guaranteed before the run started

`_aggregate.ts`'s `isHistoricalMatch` normalizes with `s.replace(/\s+/g, "")`,
which strips newlines along with every other space. Every side collapses to a
single line. The `compose` branch's shared-line overlap test — "if both sides
have ≥1 shared line of length >3" — therefore degenerates to string equality,
and the whole grader reduces to:

- `keep_ours` correct ⟺ `resolution == ours`
- `keep_theirs` correct ⟺ `resolution == theirs`
- `compose` correct ⟺ `resolution == ours` **or** `resolution == theirs`
- `halt` never correct

Constant `compose` is the union of the other two. **It is an upper bound on
every arm, for every corpus, by construction.** `arm only = 0` is not a
measurement; it is arithmetic. An exhaustive check over synthetic inputs finds
zero cases where any decision is correct while constant `compose` is wrong
(`packages/core/tests/m1a/h0-evidence-scripts.test.ts`, "grader ceiling").

On this corpus the ceiling bites hard: the developer's resolution equals `ours`
or `theirs` verbatim on 9 of 30 triples. **The other 21 are unwinnable by any
arm** — no decision in the enum `{keep_ours, keep_theirs, compose, halt}` can
score correct on a genuinely blended resolution, because the grader has no way
to recognize a blend.

So H0's acceptance gate is unsatisfiable in the same structural sense the
original kill condition was (`reference/self-attack.md`: "H0's kill condition
met: false from the original run was unfalsifiable (Infinity)"). That defect
was removed from the kill rule and left in place in the grader.

**What this null does and does not license.** It licenses: "this adjudicator,
on this corpus, under this grader, did not beat a constant." It does not
license "evidence-conditioned adjudication does not work" — the instrument
could not have shown that it does.

## Characterizations (reported, not binding)

Two favorable readings exist. Both are recorded so they stay checkable, and
neither is the gate.

1. **Wrong-count.** Full-bundle makes 49 wrong decisions to compose's 63. It
   buys that entirely by declining to answer 27 of 90 times, while being
   significantly *worse* on correctness (p = 0.0001). A policy that halts on
   everything scores `wrong = 0` and would win this comparison outright. WP4's
   STOP rule names this failure mode: "if forced resolution dominates halt at
   any plausible cost ratio."
2. **Break-even halt cost.** On records valid in both arms, an arm is cheaper
   than `baseline-compose` when one halt costs less than this fraction of one
   wrong merge:

   | arm | paired records | arm wrong | arm halt | compose wrong | wrong avoided | break-even halt:wrong |
   | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
   | hunk-only | 86 | 56 | 10 | 62 | 6 | 0.600 |
   | selected | 87 | 53 | 12 | 60 | 7 | 0.583 |
   | full-bundle | 89 | 49 | 27 | 62 | 13 | 0.481 |
   | *degenerate: halt on everything* | *90* | *0* | *90* | *63* | *63* | ***0.700*** |

   **Read the last row before quoting any of the others.** A policy that halts
   on every record makes zero wrong decisions and scores 0.700 on this same
   measure — higher than all three arms. So these ratios rank every arm *below*
   a policy that never decides anything. That is the measure paying for
   declining to answer, the same defect as the wrong-fraction gate, and it is
   why the ratios are reported rather than used to close the phase. The
   earlier 10.060 pp wrong-fraction figure carries the same label in
   `current-state.md` and is also the wrong comparison: it measures full-bundle
   against *hunk-only*, another model arm, not against the trivial baseline the
   gate names.

## Validity is a separate gate, and it did not change

The fabrication rule (`INVALIDATION-RULE.md`) still fires on this run
(`selected`: 2, `full-bundle`: 1), so `verdict` remains `invalid` in the
artifact, and the human review of 2026-08-30 still overrides that to VALID on
the grounds that the three flagged citations are tag *formatting*. None of that
is touched here.

The acceptance-gate outcome is recorded separately as `significance_verdict`
so a run reviewed back to valid cannot silently inherit a verdict it never
earned. **Valid ≠ positive.** The run was clean; the hypothesis did not hold.
